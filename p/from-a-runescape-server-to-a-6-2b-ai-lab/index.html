Funding

From a RuneScape server to a $6.2B AI lab.

At 14 he started his own server for a video game. Now gamers’ clips train his $6.2B AI lab.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 6, 2026, 12:30 a.m. PT

Pim de Witte, who is Dutch, started Soul Split, a private RuneScape server, at 14, GamesBeat reported; TechCrunch says building and hosting a private RuneScape server made him $1.5M in his teens. In 2018 he co-founded Medal, an app where gamers save and share clips. Its sister company, General Intuition, trains AI on the button presses recorded with those clips, to act in games and steer robots.

The twist: in late 2024, OpenAI tried to buy Medal for $500M, The Information reported. It didn’t sell.

Since then: a $133.7M seed in Oct 2025, $320M at $2.3B in a round closed in January, and on Sept 29, $220M at $6.2B, led by Valor and Atreides, per AlleyWatch. Its MIRA model is still a research demo, SiliconANGLE notes.

Our take: look at the data your product already throws off.
