Should an algorithm be allowed to fire you?

California just banned firing workers on an algorithm’s say-so alone, unless they’re gig workers.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 4, 2026, 4:30 a.m. PT

On Sept 30, Gov. Gavin Newsom signed SB 947 by state Sen. Jerry McNerney. Starting July 1, 2027, employers can’t fire or discipline a worker based solely on an algorithm. If an algorithm drives the decision, a person must check it against records, reviews or interviews. The fine: $500 a violation. “Nobody wants to be fired by a machine,” McNerney told KQED.

The final law dropped contractors and gig workers, CalMatters reports. Amazon Flex drivers, “fired by bot” in a 2021 Bloomberg story, stay outside it.

Even GOP gubernatorial nominee Steve Hilton said “the unions got it right,” per the Sacramento Bee. Meanwhile, 26 Meta workers sued, alleging AI rankings picked people on medical or maternity leave for layoffs. Meta denies it: decisions “were and are made by people, not AI.”

Newsom signed it one day after Trump signed a voluntary AI accord with tech CEOs.

Our take: a floor, not a fix. $500 won’t scare a big employer. But the default is right: software can flag a worker. Only a person should fire one.

On the record Oct 3, 2026

By Dec 31, 2027, at least 3 more states sign laws barring employers from firing workers based solely on AI.
