SpaceX alums built a self-flying plane startup. It’s now worth $1B+.

Swipe to see the SpaceX alums teaching planes to fly themselves.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 21, 2026, 10 a.m. PT

Reliable Robotics, a Mountain View, California startup, raised a $115 million extension of its Series D: $90 million in equity led by Nimble Partners and $25 million in debt from Silicon Valley Bank. The Series D now totals $275 million, total funding is $380 million, and the company is valued at more than $1 billion.

Its system, built to be installed on existing aircraft, keeps the autopilot on through taxi, takeoff and landing, with a pilot aboard using a touchscreen. CEO Robert Rose expects FAA certification in 18 to 24 months. Flights run remotely from a ground station are at least three years away, he says.

Rose was director of flight software at SpaceX, and co-founder and CTO Juerg Frefel spent nearly a decade as a SpaceX engineer. Reliable Airlines, based in Albuquerque, flies cargo in six Cessna Caravans six days a week and aims to be the first airline certified to use the technology.
