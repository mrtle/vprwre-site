The paper that exposed Epstein is betting on AI?

Four months after a Pulitzer citation for its Epstein reporting, the Miami Herald lost about 30 jobs.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 5, 2026, 4:30 a.m. PT

On Sept 10, owner McClatchy laid off 90+ union journalists at 17 papers, about 30% of its union staff, AP reported. Herald counts vary by outlet. McClatchy frames the cuts around “subscriber interests.” Critics tie them to its AI push.

Since March, McClatchy’s AI tool has rewritten reporters’ stories into new versions that run under their bylines. In April, 4 of McClatchy’s top 5 articles were AI rewrites, Straight Arrow News (SAN) reported. That stat has one source. “Journalists who are defiant will fall behind,” Eric Nelson, McClatchy’s VP of local news, told staff, per TheWrap.

“We don’t want to put our bylines on stories we did not actually write,” Sacramento Bee reporter Ariane Lange said, per The New York Times. Reporters withheld bylines and held a one-day strike. The Lexington Herald-Leader has two reporters left for city and state government.

Our take: a byline is a promise. Label AI copy, or don’t run it.

On the record Oct 3, 2026

By Dec 31, 2027, Gannett, Lee or Alden’s MediaNews is reported to have started running AI rewrites under reporters’ bylines after Oct 3, 2026.
