SpaceX is renting $40 billion of AI chips.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 26, 2026, 1 p.m. PT

SpaceX, Elon Musk’s now-public rocket company, told the SEC on Monday that it will lease its AI chips. On Oct. 24 it signed a binding commitment letter for a master finance lease with an entity managed by Apollo Global Management.

How it works: the Apollo-managed entity buys GPU pods and related equipment, then leases them to SpaceX, which picks the data centers. The gear arrives in batches, and each batch is leased for about five years, with rent paid every quarter, in advance. Each payment is generally 1/20 of the cost, plus interest and some ongoing costs, at a fixed rate to be set before the final lease is signed. SpaceX expects principal payments to total about $40 billion.

It isn’t final yet. The deal still has to meet closing conditions, and SpaceX expects it to close before year end. The leases are expected to start when the GPUs arrive, in the first half of 2027.

Earlier this month, Reuters and the FT reported SpaceX was in talks to raise $40 billion in debt to buy Nvidia AI chips, with Apollo expected to lead.
