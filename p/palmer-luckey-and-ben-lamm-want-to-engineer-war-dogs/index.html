Palmer Luckey and Ben Lamm want to engineer war dogs.

Palmer Luckey and Ben Lamm want to engineer war dogs.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 23, 2026, 5:15 a.m. PT

Warhound came out of stealth on Thursday with $200 million from investors including Sequoia, 8VC and In-Q-Tel, at a $1.2 billion valuation. It comes from Ben Lamm, CEO of de-extinction company Colossal Biosciences, which owns equity in Warhound and licenses it technology. Lamm says Anduril founder Palmer Luckey is a partner and doesn’t own equity.

The plan: cell lines from Belgian Malinois and German Shepherds go into a biobank, and gene edits would create “a new type of dog.” Lamm says the dogs could be enhanced for health and longevity, and for things like speed, agility or smell for bomb sniffing. Anduril will add software and hardware, like protective vests and goggles, “on top of the dog, not in the dog.” Warhound says more than 80% of the US military’s working dogs come from Europe, and less than half of them are fit for service.

The first batch is about to be cloned with help from Colossal-owned Viagen, with births expected early next year, and Lamm hopes the dogs are in service within two years. Warhound expects to sell to the Defense Department, police, first responders and military service groups, but doesn’t know yet what it will charge. The nonprofit American Humane Society is guiding how it certifies training, and retired dogs may join its Military Working Dog Reunification Program.
