Meta wants $95 billion. Zuckerberg keeps control.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 26, 2026, 11 a.m. PT

On Monday, Meta launched about $35 billion in stock sales: $22.5 billion of Class A stock and about $12.5 billion of preferred shares that later convert into common stock. It says the money supports its AI infrastructure and “personal superintelligence.”

That’s the small part. Meta plans to sell about $60 billion more through new Class C shares, starting in 2027 at the earliest, and Class C shares have no voting rights. Zuckerberg’s Class B shares get 10 votes each, and while Class B shares hold a majority of the vote, he “will effectively control” the company, the filing says.

Meta expects $135 billion to $145 billion in capital spending this year. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom, which sell AI chips, have signaled non-binding interest in buying up to $5 billion of the Class A stock.

It’s Meta’s first stock sale since 2013, Semafor reports, and bigger than Google’s $85 billion and Oracle’s $25 billion stock sales this year. Big tech companies are expected to spend $800 billion on AI this year and over $1 trillion next year, Semafor reports.
