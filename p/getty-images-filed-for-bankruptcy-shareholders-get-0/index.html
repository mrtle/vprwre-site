Getty Images filed for bankruptcy. Its shareholders get $0.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 22, 2026, 10 a.m. PT

Getty Images filed for Chapter 11 on Wednesday in Delaware for certain US entities, to carry out a prepackaged restructuring it agreed with its major financial stakeholders on Oct. 12. Members of the Getty family and affiliates of Koch Equity Development are putting in up to $533 million in new equity and will own 100% of the company when it emerges. Under the plan, existing shares are canceled and stockholders receive nothing.

How it got here: Getty’s bankruptcy disclosure points to more than $200 million in judgments and interest from lawsuits brought by holders of its stock warrants, about $239 million a year in debt service, and the end of its $3.7 billion merger with Shutterstock after UK regulators required Shutterstock to sell its editorial business. The plan eliminates more than $500 million of debt, and holders of $270 million in unsecured notes are set to recover about 63% in cash.

Getty, whose library holds more than 662 million images and videos, says it expects no impact on customers, contributors, employees or vendors, and it aims to finish by year end. CEO Craig Peters called the filing “a procedural step to implement the financial restructuring previously announced.”
