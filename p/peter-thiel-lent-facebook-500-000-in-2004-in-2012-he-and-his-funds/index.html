Peter Thiel lent Facebook $500,000 in 2004. In 2012, he and his funds cashed out over $1 billion.

In 2004, a PayPal co-founder lent a months-old Facebook $500,000. Then Facebook missed his target.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 6, 2026, 2 p.m. PT

The investor was Peter Thiel. The loan would turn into 10.2% of the company if the site reached 1.5 million users by December 2004. It fell short. The twist: he converted anyway, joined the board, and he and his funds put money into later rounds too.

At the May 2012 IPO, Thiel and his funds sold 16.8 million shares for about $640 million. Three months later they sold 20 million more for about $396 million: just over $1 billion in total. Fortune calculated in 2025 that those August shares would have been worth about $14.8 billion.

Our take: bet on founders, not KPIs, and know your exit plan.
