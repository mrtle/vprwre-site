They hated bad movie dubbing. Now their startup is valued at $22B.

Two high school friends from Poland hated how foreign films were dubbed. Now their AI voice startup is valued at $22 billion.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 5, 2026, 10:30 p.m. PT

Mati Staniszewski and Piotr Dąbkowski met at 15 in Warsaw and grew up watching foreign films voiced by one flat narrator for every role. “Ask any Polish person and they will tell you it’s terrible,” Mati told Forbes. In 2022 they quit Palantir and Google, pooled their savings and built ElevenLabs.

The twist: it now answers the phone. Its agents handle 15M+ conversations a week, the company says, and Klarna’s U.S. first-line phone support runs on it, TechCrunch reports.

The $22B comes from a $300M employee tender: staff sold shares to investors, so no new money went to the company.

Our take: your annoyance is a market. And the better these voices get, the harder they are to spot.
