This guy spent 30 years hunting hackers. Now he’s building AI ones.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 5, 2026, 11 p.m. PT

Kevin Mandia is a former Air Force special agent. In 2004 he founded Mandiant, a cybersecurity firm that investigates breaches. FireEye bought it for $1 billion; Google later bought the renamed Mandiant for $5.4 billion.

His new startup, Armadin, sends swarms of AI agents into companies’ own systems, with permission, to find holes before criminals do. The twist: the hacker hunter says attackers now have the edge.

In August, Armadin ran what it calls the largest controlled live AI cyberattack on record: 26,000 agents, three days. On Oct 1 it announced $255.5M at a valuation above $2.5 billion, co-led by a16z and Accel. Revenue: undisclosed.

Our take: investors bought 30 years inside real breaches. Revenue is the next proof.

Would you let AI hackers attack your company before the real ones do?

Armadin has published research on a flaw it found in Anthropic’s Claude Cowork; Anthropic disputes the risk.
