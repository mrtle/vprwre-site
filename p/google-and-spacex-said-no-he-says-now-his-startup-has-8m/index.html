Google and SpaceX said no to his idea, he says. Now his startup has $8M.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 6, 2026, 2 a.m. PT

Rama Afullo, a Kenyan-born engineer, worked at Tesla, then Google’s cloud business, then briefly at SpaceX’s Starlink in 2024. He told TechCrunch he pitched orbital data centers at Google and at SpaceX, and both said no. So he quit and started Satlyt: software that lets AI run on satellites instead of beaming every byte down to Earth.

The twist: now both are building their own. On Oct 1, Satlyt’s software launched on a SpaceX rocket, on the same flight as Google’s first Project Suncatcher prototype.

Satlyt says the AI it ran on a Momentus spacecraft shrank error-report transmissions by more than 60%. Houston’s Non Sibi Ventures led the $8M seed.

Our take: a big company’s “no” can mean early, not wrong.
