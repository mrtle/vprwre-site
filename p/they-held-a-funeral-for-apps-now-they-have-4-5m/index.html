They held a funeral for apps. Now they have $4.5M.

Two founders who quit school held a real funeral for apps. Now they have $4.5M to replace them.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 6, 2026, 12 a.m. PT

On Sept 17, their San Francisco startup, Photon, held “The APP Funeral,” with, per TechCrunch, a real coffin for app icons. Daniel Tian dropped out of Penn’s M&T program and Ryan Zhu left high school, TechCrunch reports. Their pitch: AI agents inside iMessage and WhatsApp, so you don’t need another app.

The twist: Photon’s iMessage service is backed by “a fleet of Macs that relay iMessage traffic,” its own blog says.

Photon says it has 40,000+ developer sign-ups and grew revenue 10x in four months. On Oct 1 it announced a $4.5M seed co-led by Gradient and A*. But 98% of use is still its free open-source version, TechCrunch reports.

Our take: don’t explain your thesis. Stage it.
