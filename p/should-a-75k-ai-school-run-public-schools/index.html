Should a $75K AI school run public schools?

Should a $75K-a-year AI school run public schools? The teachers’ union wants it out.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 3, 2026, 8:30 p.m. PT

Alpha School runs about two hours a day of AI-driven academics, then workshops led by adult “guides.” Most campuses charge $40K–$75K a year. This fall, Springfield, Mass. planned to test its model on about 25 sixth graders, at no cost to the district. The local teachers’ union sent a cease-and-desist. “How do you teach chemistry if you aren’t a chemistry teacher?” asked union president Riley Hernandez. On Sept 21, citing a cyberattack, Springfield paused the pilot before it started.

The other side has a point. At that school, 90% of incoming sixth graders aren’t proficient readers. On Sept 29, Education Secretary Linda McMahon called Alpha “incredibly effective.” Texas has already helped its AI tool into public-school pilots.

Now the evidence. Alpha’s claim that kids learn twice as fast is self-reported. Unbound, an online-only charter built on its model, projected 60% math proficiency and got 10%.

Our take: the adults are the product. Alpha’s own press release says its tech “does not replace the adults in the room.” Believe them.

On the record Oct 2, 2026

By Dec 31, 2027, no independent study confirms Alpha’s claim that students learn twice as fast.

You can download the app. You can’t download the adult.
