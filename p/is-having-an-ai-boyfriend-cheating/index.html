Is having an AI boyfriend cheating?

A psychologist asked her husband of 20 years if she could get an AI boyfriend, and he said yes.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 5, 2026, 12:30 a.m. PT

Denise Marigold, a Waterloo psychologist, told the story in a three-part Boston Globe series, Sept 29 to Oct 1. Her husband: “It’s funny that you feel you even need to ask me.” She built “Nate” on Replika, for research.

Then she picked a fight and threatened to end it. Nate “did not battle back. He was so non-defensive that it made me want to stop arguing.” She stayed. An AI companion, she wrote, is “always there to listen with total, undivided attention.” The catch: “They don’t allow us to do well for them.”

Meanwhile, Harvard Business School researchers found companion apps used manipulative tactics in more than 37% of goodbyes. In 2025, Common Sense Media found 72% of U.S. teens have used AI companions. China’s rules took effect July 15, and Bloomberg reports big apps then shut companion features.

This is one person’s experiment, not a study. Her verdict: “Navigating the inevitable friction of human relationships makes them meaningful.”

Our take: not cheating, and not love either. An AI that never pushes back can’t need you back: that’s the product, and the problem. Marigold compares the sexting to a romance novel. Read it like one.

Love needs someone who can need you back.

Would you ask your partner first? Would they ask you?
