Microsoft offered about 60,000 workers money to quit

Microsoft just offered about 60,000 employees money to leave.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 27, 2026, 11 a.m. PT

On Monday, Microsoft introduced a “Voluntary Exit Offer,” a time-limited program that lets eligible employees leave with “financial support and career planning resources.” The company confirmed to TechCrunch that about 60,000 employees are eligible. That’s more than 1 in 4 of the roughly 223,000 people Microsoft employed full-time at the end of June.

Who’s eligible: employees at level 70 and below on participating teams in the US, Canada, India and the UK. Xbox, Windows, Copilot and Finance are among the divisions taking part, Bloomberg reports. Some employees, including those in data centers, sales and marketing, are excluded, according to TechCrunch. TechCrunch reports the terms are similar to Microsoft’s standard severance.

“The Voluntary Exit Offer is new for us. We’ll learn from it,” wrote Amy Coleman, Microsoft’s chief people officer.

It follows Microsoft’s first-ever voluntary retirement program earlier this year, when about 30% of the roughly 8,750 eligible US workers accepted.

The backdrop: Microsoft spent $115.9 billion on property and equipment in the fiscal year that ended in June, up from $64.6 billion the year before. TechCrunch calls the buyouts part of an effort to cut costs and rework the workforce for the AI age.
