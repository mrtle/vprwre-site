India just blocked an app that works without the internet.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 6, 2026, 4 p.m. PT

It’s Bitchat, a messaging app from Jack Dorsey. Phones swap encrypted messages over Bluetooth, with no accounts or servers. In July, student protesters in Delhi turned to it when mobile internet was suspended nearby.

In a notice Dorsey posted Oct. 3, Apple said India’s IT ministry demanded the takedown under Section 69A. The app is also gone from Google Play in India. India appears to be only the second country, after China, where Apple has pulled it, per MediaNama.

The order hasn’t been published, and the government hasn’t given a public reason. Apple, Google and the ministry didn’t respond to TechCrunch’s requests for comment.

Our take: you can pull an app from a store, but not from the phones that already have it. As offline apps become protest tools, app stores are becoming the pressure point.
