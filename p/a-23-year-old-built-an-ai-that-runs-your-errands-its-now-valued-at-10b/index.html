A 23-year-old built an AI that runs your errands. Investors just valued it at $10 billion.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 6, 2026, 2:30 a.m. PT

It’s called Instinct. You text or call it, and it uses its own phone and computer to finish everyday tasks: trips, groceries, cancellations. Its founder, Noah Shinn, was first author of an AI research paper as an undergrad, then an early employee at Bret Taylor’s startup Sierra.

The twist: Shinn says Instinct has spent $0 on marketing. Instinct isn’t broadly available yet, and he says invites are being resold on eBay.

In late August it raised $250 million at a $2.5 billion valuation, The Wall Street Journal reported. On Sept. 28 it announced $1 billion from Sequoia, Benchmark and Coatue at $10 billion.

But in August, TechCrunch reported testers’ privacy complaints, including terms granting a perpetual license to user materials. Instinct said it was taking the concerns seriously.

Our take: trust is the product. An agent that holds your inbox and your card can’t afford a single privacy slip.
