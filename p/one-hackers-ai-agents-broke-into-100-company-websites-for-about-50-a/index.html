One hacker’s AI agents broke into more than 100 company websites, for about $50 a hack.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 20, 2026, 6:11 a.m. PT

Starting Sept. 25, a Chinese-speaking hacker pointed AI agents at more than 1,200 websites in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan and left almost all of the work to them. Security firm NorForge uncovered the operation because the hackers had left their own control panels open on the internet.

The agents ran on Cairn, an open-source AI tool whose creator says it “was not designed for hacking attacks.” NorForge researcher Snorre Fagerland estimates the whole campaign cost about $5,000 in AI fees, roughly $50 per successful hack. A successful break-in took about 23 hours on average.

Websites tied to John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins were among those hit. Deere says it has “no indication that sensitive information was accessed,” SharkNinja says the threat to a standalone product-support site is contained, and Cummins says it is investigating a report involving a site run by one of its vendors.

“It’s a new world now,” Fagerland said. More on AI and cybersecurity at vprwre.com.
