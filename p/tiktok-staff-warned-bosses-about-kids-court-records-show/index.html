TikTok staff warned bosses about kids, court records show.

TikTok staff warned bosses about risks to kids, court records show.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 21, 2026, 6 p.m. PT

NPR reviewed depositions from TikTok employees, filed in federal court in March in a multidistrict case against TikTok, Meta, Google and Snap over alleged harms to users under 18. Employees said they repeatedly raised concerns about risks to children, including sexual exploitation and exposure to suicide content, and were frustrated with how leadership responded.

Among the details: an internal investigation found children aged 13 to 15 in 112,000 TikTok Live videos in January 2022 alone, according to testimony. In 2018, a user survey avoided questions that could reveal users’ ages. “If the FTC knows about this survey, they might want us to submit the results,” then-head of TikTok Live Adam Wang wrote in an internal chat. A 2021 chat said teen accounts had a hidden link to unlink from parents’ “family pairing” controls; TikTok says it notifies parents when a teen unlinks.

TikTok spokesperson Nathaniel Brown said the citations and claims are “clearly cherry-picked at the behest of trial lawyers and used to inaccurately rewrite our history and mislead the public about our commitment to youth safety and how our platform works.” The depositions haven’t been submitted as evidence at trial.
