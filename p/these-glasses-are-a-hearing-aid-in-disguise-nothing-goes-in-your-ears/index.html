These glasses are a hearing aid in disguise. Nothing goes in your ears.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 6, 2026, 3:30 a.m. PT

Legato Frames launched in the U.S. on Oct. 1. On-device AI in the frame picks voices out of background noise and plays them through tiny open-ear speakers. There are no cameras, and nothing is recorded.

The twist: no hearing exam needed. Legato sells them as FDA-regulated over-the-counter hearing aids for adults with mild-to-moderate hearing loss. Prices start at $999, and they take prescription lenses.

The Massachusetts startup is led by ex-Bose execs, including the creator of Bose Frames, and has raised $12 million. Eye-care clinics in select states start selling them later this month.

Our take: hearing aids spent decades trying to be invisible. Legato is betting on glasses people actually want to wear.
