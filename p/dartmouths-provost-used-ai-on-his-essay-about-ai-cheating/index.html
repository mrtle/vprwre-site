Dartmouth’s provost used AI on his essay about AI cheating.

Dartmouth’s provost wrote an essay about AI cheating. The detector Pangram scored it “100% AI-written,” per Semafor. He says he wrote it from his own thinking and used ChatGPT to refine a few arguments.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 5, 2026, 6 p.m. PT

Santiago Schnell’s Aug 9 Washington Post column argued degrees should separate students’ own work from AI’s. The Dartmouth, the student paper, then tested nine pieces he published this year: median 96% AI-written. He declined to share his AI logs.

The twist: five of his papers from before ChatGPT scored 100% human.

A 2025 University of Chicago study put the detector’s error rate near zero; a 2023 Stanford study found older detectors misflag non-native English writers, and Schnell says he is one. On Sept 25 he apologized for not disclosing his AI use, and a review was ordered.

Our take: using AI isn’t the problem; not disclosing it is. Same rule for a provost and a freshman.

Where does editing end and writing begin?

Dartmouth has a campus AI partnership with Anthropic and AWS; Schnell says he used ChatGPT.
