Zuckerberg’s plan to pay a billionaire tax and keep control.

Zuckerberg’s plan to pay a billionaire tax and keep control.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 23, 2026, 12 p.m. PT

Meta’s board approved a new class of nonvoting stock, called Class C common stock, which takes effect in January. Each Class A and Class B share gets one Class C share in a one-time dividend, described as a two-for-one split, which Meta says “will not change the voting power of any shareholder.” The new shares can also be used to raise capital, pay employees and make acquisitions.

The New York Times reported the plan would help Zuckerberg pay California’s proposed billionaire tax, Proposition 40, if voters approve it, without losing control of Meta, where he holds around 60% of the vote. Meta’s filing allows him to immediately sell or transfer up to 20% of the Class C shares he receives, about 10% of his overall economic stake. It’s been estimated he could owe as much as $11 billion.

Prop 40, drafted by Berkeley tax law professors and sponsored by SEIU United Healthcare Workers West, would be a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of several hundred California billionaires. Backers say it could raise up to $100 billion over five years, mostly for healthcare, with the rest for food assistance and education. Google co-founder Sergey Brin has campaigned against it, and other critics say it would drive capital out of the state. Rep. Ro Khanna, a Democrat who backs the tax, wrote on X: “Looks like billionaires will stay and figure out a way to pay the tax.”

According to the Times, Zuckerberg told Meta’s board in September that if they couldn’t reach an agreement, he’d list his Palo Alto property for sale. Meta’s special committee said it was important for him to keep leading the company from California, including “in close proximity to Meta Superintelligence Labs.”
