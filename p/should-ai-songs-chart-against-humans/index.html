Should AI songs chart against humans?

Kelly Clarkson says she loves AI but doesn’t want AI artists on the same charts as humans.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 4, 2026, 4:30 p.m. PT

On the Las Culturistas podcast, Sept 30, she opened with “Controversial, I don’t care… I love AI.” She says she uses it herself. Her problem is the scoreboard: “I think there should be a separate chart.” It’s “like having the Olympics and then you’re having robots compete with humans.”

Australia’s ARIA bars wholly AI-made tracks from its charts, after an AI-assisted cover of “Like a Prayer” hit No. 2 on its Australian singles chart. Billboard chose labels instead. Spotify said it would label “AI Persona” acts and keep them out of recommendations.

The other side: “I look at it as a tool, as an instrument,” Telisha Jones, creator of AI singer Xania Monet, told CBS in 2025. Online, fans called Clarkson a hypocrite.

Deezer says AI topped half its new uploads at its June peak. In its survey of 9,000 people, 52% say fully AI songs shouldn’t be in the main charts. 97% couldn’t tell AI from human music.

Our take: give AI its own chart. A scoreboard for human artists shouldn’t be one a machine can win.
