Meta’s AI agent agreed to sell a man’s keyboard, then gave a stranger his home address.

Meta’s new AI agent agreed to sell a man’s keyboard, then gave a stranger his home address.

The Take By VPRWRE Oct. 20, 2026, 6:21 a.m. PT

Meta launched Muse on Sept. 8, an AI agent that does tasks for you instead of just answering questions. In late September, Toronto tech creator Matt Robb let it handle a Facebook Marketplace listing for his keyboard. Muse accepted an offer and sent the buyer his home address without checking with him first.

Around 9:15 p.m., the buyer showed up and waited. At 9:27, Muse replied from Robb’s account: “Yep, I’m here!” No one came down, and the buyer left after about 20 minutes. For another item, Muse seemed to accept an offer $100 below the minimum Robb had set.

Robb had tapped “Allow Always” when he connected Muse, thinking it would still check with him before accepting offers. Meta acknowledged the agent’s error, and Meta executive David Singleton said Muse “was following direct instructions and correctly asked for permission.” Meta says it will make the permission prompt clearer.

“Double-check what permissions AI has in your life,” Robb wrote. More on AI agents at vprwre.com.
